## 1. Assumptions

User engagement is held constant across scales to isolate volume effects. MAU and per-user engagement are treated as independent metrics.

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Messages/user/day | 8 (fixed) | Independence of MAU and engagement |
| Input tokens/message | 2,000 (500 user + 1,500 RAG) | Academic use with PDF uploads and RAG |
| Output tokens/message | 400 | Explanatory responses in educational contexts |
| RAG trigger rate | 50% of messages | Mixed direct and document-grounded queries |
| File uploads | 5 MB/user/month | Study PDFs, lecture notes |
| Retention policy | Semester-scoped (6 months) | GDPR (EU right to erasure), data isolation, pedagogical relevance |
| DeepSeek pricing | $0.14/$0.28 per 1M tokens (in/out) | Verify at [platform.deepseek.com](https://platform.deepseek.com); pricing has changed previously (Sep 2025) |

**Qdrant corpus sizing (bottom-up):** 20 courses/year × 24 documents/course (12 slide decks + 8 readings + 4 misc) = 480 documents. At ~13 chunks of 512 tokens each = ~6,250 vectors. At 768 dimensions (float32) with ~10× HNSW index overhead: **~150–200 MB total**. Corpus size is driven by institutional content, not cumulative student uploads — the semester-scoped retention policy prunes user documents each term. If retention drifts to indefinite accumulation, 1,000 users over 3 years could generate ~11.7 GB, fundamentally changing Qdrant's role as a cost driver.

## 3. Top 3 Cost Drivers at 1,000 MAU

**1. EC2 compute — $121 (39%).** Fixed cost for hosting 9 containers, independent of user activity.

**2. DeepSeek API — $94 (30%).** The only truly variable cost, scaling linearly with usage. Despite 240,000 messages/month, DeepSeek's aggressive pricing keeps this under $100.

**3. RDS Multi-AZ — $56 (18%).** A compliance-driven availability requirement that costs more than LLM inference at 10 and 100 MAU.

**Structural insight:** At this operating point, fixed infrastructure accounts for ~70% of spend. The cost structure is infrastructure-driven, not AI-driven — a counterintuitive finding for an "AI product."

**However, this finding is conditional on the specific premises** (DeepSeek pricing, 1,000 MAU, simple RAG workload, bounded corpus). The sensitivity is high: switching to GPT-4o ($2.50/$10.00 per 1M tokens) would push the LLM line to ~$1,560/month, making AI 80%+ of total spend. Adopting agentic workflows (5–10× token amplification) achieves the same inversion even on DeepSeek. Increasing average context to 50,000+ tokens per query multiplies input cost by 25×.

**Any single parameter change is sufficient to flip the cost structure.** The robust conclusion: at this specific operating point, infrastructure dominates; but the crossover is fragile. The school's LLM procurement decision is the single highest-leverage cost variable — more impactful than any AWS choice. DeepSeek's pricing may not persist (Sep 2025 restructuring precedent), introducing vendor concentration risk the school cannot control.

## 4. Two Cost-Cutting Levers

### Lever 1: Reserved Instances (~21% saving)

Convert EC2 and RDS to 1-year No Upfront RIs (~38% discount; verify at [aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/reserved-instances](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/reserved-instances/)):

| Component | On-demand | 1yr RI | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| EC2 t3.xlarge | $121 | ~$75 | ~$46 |
| RDS db.t3.small Multi-AZ | $53 | ~$33 | ~$20 |
| **Total** | | | **~$66 (21%)** |

**SLO trade-off:** No performance degradation. The cost is 12-month lock-in: if enrolment drops (1,000 → 200 MAU), the school overpays ~$42/month for the remaining term (~$500 worst case). For an institution with predictable annual enrolment cycles, this risk is low.

### Lever 2: Reduce availability tier (~16% saving)

Downgrade RDS to Single-AZ (saving ~$27.50) and replace ALB with Caddy on EC2 (saving ~$22). Combined: **~$49.50 (15.8%)**.

**This optimisation consciously compromises production stability and horizontal scalability for cost savings.** It contradicts the Q2 architecture, which specified ALB + ACM for reliability, certificate management, health checks, and scaling readiness — benefits that are valuable regardless of user count, not just at scale.

**SLO trade-off (quantified):** Single-AZ RDS increases RTO from <60 seconds (automatic failover) to 15–30 minutes (manual recovery). Expected annual downtime rises from ~2 minutes to ~45–90 minutes. Removing ALB creates a single point of failure and eliminates horizontal scaling without re-architecting. For an internal academic tool (99.5% uptime tolerance vs 99.95%), this trade-off is defensible outside peak periods — though exam-week outages could affect dozens of students.

**Both levers target infrastructure** because that's where the money is at this operating point. LLM token optimisation would save at most ~$30–40/month — below the 15% threshold. If the cost premises change (premium LLM, agentic workflows), levers must shift to AI-focused strategies.

## 5. Unit Economics

| Scale | Total cost | Cost/user/month |
|---|---|---|
| 10 MAU | ~$149 | **$14.90** |
| 100 MAU | ~$161 | **$1.61** |
| 1,000 MAU | ~$313 | **$0.31** |

Fixed costs (~$146 at t3.large tier, ~$213 at t3.xlarge) dominate. Variable cost is ~$0.10/user/month. The formula: **Cost/user = (Fixed ÷ MAU) + $0.10**.

**Where does the curve break?** Two regimes: a steep decline from 10–200 MAU (per-user cost drops 18× from $14.70 to $0.83, driven by fixed-cost amortisation), then flattening from 200–1,000 MAU as per-user cost approaches the ~$0.25–0.30 asymptote. The **practical break point is ~200–300 MAU** — below this, the product pays primarily for infrastructure per user; above it, further growth yields diminishing improvement. A step-function discontinuity occurs at the EC2 tier boundary (~500 MAU), where the $60 instance upgrade momentarily increases per-user cost before the curve resumes declining.

## 6. Pricing Recommendation

At 100 MAU: cost/user = $1.61. For 50% gross margin: **$1.61 ÷ 0.50 = $3.22/user/month** (~€2.95).

This is competitive: ChatGPT Plus costs $20/month, GitHub Copilot $10/month. A school-branded RAG-enabled assistant at <€3/month offers institution-specific value (course-grounded responses, EU data residency) at a fraction of commercial alternatives.

**Recommended structure:** €20/student/semester (~$3.33/month effective). At 100 MAU this yields ~52% margin; at 1,000 MAU, ~91% margin as infrastructure amortises. Absolute profit at 1,000 MAU (~$36,000/year) is modest — viable as an embedded institutional service, positioned as cost avoidance versus $10–20/user/month commercial AI licences rather than standalone revenue generation.

**Adoption risk:** This recommendation assumes the school achieves and sustains sufficient adoption to reach the flat segment of the unit economics curve (~200–300 MAU). If adoption stalls at 50–100 MAU, the per-user cost remains $1.61–2.92 — still serviceable at the €20/semester price point, but with margins compressed to 52% or below and absolute profit too thin to justify ongoing operational investment. The economics are most compelling at scale, which means the school must treat adoption as a strategic priority, not an afterthought: embedding the tool in course syllabi, providing faculty training, and integrating with existing LMS workflows. Without deliberate adoption effort, the fixed-cost structure that makes this product cheap at scale also makes it expensive at low adoption — the same structural characteristic that creates the opportunity also creates the risk.
