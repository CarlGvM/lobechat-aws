# Q1 — Use Case Design: AI-Powered Research Assistant for Graduate Business Education

## Context

**Industry:** Higher Education — Graduate Business School  
**Organisation:** A mid-sized European business school (500–2,000 postgraduate students) offering MBA, MSc, and executive education programmes. Think ESADE, IESE, or HEC Paris.  
**Problem today:** Students and faculty rely on a fragmented set of tools to conduct research: they query ChatGPT or Claude.ai through consumer accounts, search institutional library databases separately, manage documents in personal cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive), and communicate via email or WhatsApp. There is no single AI-assisted workspace that integrates the institution's proprietary resources — course materials, case study libraries, research paper repositories, faculty notes, and career services data.

The core pain points are:
1. **Data leakage risk**: Students paste confidential case study content and unpublished faculty research into consumer LLM interfaces, violating the school's data governance policy and potentially breaching copyright agreements with case publishers (Harvard Business Publishing, INSEAD, etc.).
2. **Fragmented context**: Every conversation starts from zero. Students cannot build on prior sessions, reference uploaded PDFs inline, or share verified AI-assisted research with faculty.
3. **No institutional accountability**: The school cannot audit which AI tools are being used, for what purpose, or at what quality level. There is no way to enforce academic integrity standards on AI usage.

Self-hosting LobeChat on AWS addresses all three pain points simultaneously.

## Personas

### Persona 1 — Ana, MSc Business Analytics Student (Primary User)

Ana is a second-year student working on her thesis, which analyses the impact of ESG reporting requirements on European SME financing. She accesses LobeChat daily from her laptop and mobile.

**Workflow today:** Ana opens four browser tabs — the library search engine, Google Scholar, Claude.ai (consumer tier), and her university VPN to access EBSCO. She manually copies abstracts into Claude, re-explains her context every session, and saves outputs in a personal Google Doc. She worries about pasting draft thesis sections into a consumer AI.

**Pain points:** Context lost between sessions. Cannot reference library PDFs directly. Unsure if using consumer AI violates the school's academic integrity policy. No structured way to cite AI assistance.

### Persona 2 — Professor Martí, Faculty Researcher (Secondary User)

Martí leads the school's sustainability research centre. He supervises 12 thesis students and produces 3–4 working papers per year.

**Workflow today:** Martí reviews student thesis drafts in Word, leaves comments, and separately queries academic databases. He uses Zotero for citation management. He has no visibility into whether students are using AI tools and, if so, how.

**Pain points:** Time-consuming to give personalised research feedback at scale. Cannot efficiently cross-reference student queries against approved sources. Wants students to use AI responsibly but lacks tooling to enforce or monitor this.

### Persona 3 — Career Services Coordinator, Laia (Tertiary User)

Laia manages job placement support for 400 students per cohort. She maintains a knowledge base of company profiles, alumni interview feedback, and coaching frameworks.

**Workflow today:** Students email Laia individually. She manually points them to documents on SharePoint. She spends 30% of her time answering repetitive questions about application timelines, company culture, and CV formatting.

**Pain points:** Repetitive, low-value queries consume most of her time. The knowledge base is not searchable by students directly. Career documents are updated infrequently because the process is cumbersome.

## Journey: Ana Writing Her ESG Thesis

This journey demonstrates the full stack in action — RAG retrieval, MCP tool invocation, model routing, and access control — all within a single LobeChat session.

### Step 1 — Authentication (Casdoor SSO)

Ana navigates to `https://ai.businessschool.edu`. She is redirected to the institution's Casdoor-managed SSO page, authenticates with her university Microsoft 365 credentials via OAuth2 OIDC, and lands on her personalised LobeChat workspace. Her identity is tied to her student role, which grants access to the student knowledge base but not to faculty-only research archives.

**Data flow:** Casdoor issues a JWT token; LobeChat receives it and loads Ana's conversation history and knowledge base permissions from PostgreSQL.

### Step 2 — RAG Query Against Institutional Knowledge Base

Ana types: *"Summarise the key arguments in the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive and how it affects SMEs under 250 employees."*

LobeChat routes this to the **Hayhooks RAG pipeline**. The pipeline generates an embedding of Ana's query using a lightweight embedding model (e.g., `text-embedding-3-small` via OpenRouter) and performs a nearest-neighbour search against the Qdrant vector store. Qdrant holds chunked and embedded versions of:
- Faculty-curated PDFs uploaded to MinIO (EU regulatory documents, working papers)
- Case study summaries licensed by the library (metadata only, not full text, respecting copyright)
- Ana's own previously uploaded thesis notes

Qdrant returns the top-5 most semantically relevant chunks. These are injected into the system prompt as grounded context before the LLM call is made.

**MCP tool fired:** `qdrant-retrieval` via MCPHub — searches the vector index and returns source-attributed chunks.

**Model handling this step:** A mid-tier instruction-following model (e.g., `mistral-medium` via OpenRouter) generates the grounded summary. Token cost is kept low because the answer is factual synthesis, not creative generation.

### Step 3 — Follow-up with Live Research Data

Ana follows up: *"Are there any recent news articles from 2025 about SME compliance costs under CSRD?"*

This triggers a second MCP tool: the **web search MCP server** (configured in MCPHub to query a news API). The tool returns recent articles. LobeChat's agent framework automatically invokes this tool because the query requires information beyond the static knowledge base.

**Data flow:** MCPHub proxies the request to the configured news API endpoint; results are returned as structured JSON, rendered inline in the chat as cited cards.

### Step 4 — Document Upload and Inline Analysis

Ana uploads a 40-page PDF draft of her thesis chapter. The file is stored in MinIO (S3-compatible object storage on the same EC2). LobeChat's multimodal pipeline chunks the PDF, generates embeddings, and temporarily indexes them in Qdrant under Ana's personal namespace.

Ana asks: *"Does my argument in section 3 contradict the CSRD position I summarised earlier?"*

The agent retrieves the relevant thesis chunks alongside the CSRD summary chunks and passes both to the LLM for cross-referencing.

**Model handling this step:** A larger context model (e.g., `claude-3-5-sonnet` via OpenRouter) is selected automatically because the task requires long-context reasoning across two document sources. OpenRouter's model routing API makes this transparent to the user.

### Step 5 — Faculty Visibility (Martí's View)

Because Ana's thesis is tagged as supervised by Professor Martí, he can open the same LobeChat workspace and view a read-only audit trail of Ana's AI-assisted research sessions: which sources were retrieved, which prompts were used, and what the model responded. He can leave a comment directly on a specific exchange.

**MCP tool fired:** `postgres-read` — retrieves conversation logs filtered by student/supervisor relationship stored in PostgreSQL.

### Step 6 — Career Services Bot (Laia's Configuration)

Separately, Laia has configured a dedicated LobeChat agent called "Career Assistant" trained on her SharePoint knowledge base (exported and ingested into Qdrant). Students can query this agent for job search advice 24/7. Laia monitors usage through LobeChat's built-in analytics and updates the knowledge base monthly by re-running the Hayhooks ingestion pipeline against the updated MinIO bucket.

**Measurable value:** In a pilot at a comparable institution, AI-assisted career services reduced average response time from 3 days to under 2 minutes for 80% of routine queries (based on general benchmarks from similar chatbot deployments in higher education; verify against current sources for precise figures).

## Why This Stack vs ChatGPT Enterprise / Microsoft Copilot

### Reason 1 — Data Sovereignty and Institutional IP Control

ChatGPT Enterprise and Microsoft Copilot both process data on US-based infrastructure under their own data processing agreements. For a European institution handling student personal data (GDPR-regulated), unpublished research, and licensed case study content, this creates two hard problems: (a) data residency requirements — the institution cannot guarantee that student data remains in the EU; (b) copyright exposure — pasting licensed case study content into a third-party LLM may breach the school's licence agreements with Harvard Business Publishing or INSEAD.

The self-hosted LobeChat stack runs entirely within `eu-west-1` (Ireland), inside the institution's AWS account. Data never leaves the EU region. The institution retains full control of what is stored, for how long, and who can access it.

### Reason 2 — Custom RAG Against Proprietary Knowledge Bases

Neither ChatGPT Enterprise nor Microsoft Copilot supports deep integration with arbitrary institutional knowledge bases at the retrieval level. ChatGPT's "custom knowledge" feature supports a limited file upload mechanism; Copilot integrates with Microsoft 365 content but not with external databases, licensed PDFs, or custom structured data.

The LobeChat stack includes Qdrant (vector search), Hayhooks (RAG pipeline orchestration), and MCPHub (tool routing) as first-class components. The institution can ingest any data source — EU regulatory PDFs, case study metadata, alumni placement data, career coaching frameworks — and make it retrievable via semantic search within chat. This is not achievable with SaaS alternatives without significant custom engineering on top of expensive APIs.

### Reason 3 — MCP Tool Ecosystem and Extensibility

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) layer in this stack allows the institution to expose its own internal systems as tools callable from within a chat conversation — the library catalogue API, the student information system, the career placement database, the thesis submission portal. A student could, in a single conversation, check their thesis submission deadline, retrieve relevant library articles, and draft a response to supervisor feedback — all grounded in real institutional data.

ChatGPT's plugin ecosystem and Copilot's connector framework both impose vendor-controlled constraints on what tools can be exposed, require API-level compliance with platform policies, and charge per-tool usage fees. The MCP approach is open-source and institution-controlled: any internal system with an HTTP API can become a tool at zero marginal licence cost.
