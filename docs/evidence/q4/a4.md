# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Chief Technology Officer  
**From:** Head of Digital Innovation  
**Date:** 2 June 2026  
**Subject:** Self-Hosted LobeChat vs SaaS AI Platforms — Strategic Recommendation for 200-User Deployment

---

## TL;DR

- Self-hosting LobeChat on AWS is **40–60% cheaper** than ChatGPT Enterprise or Microsoft Copilot at 200 users over three years, while giving us full control over data, models, and integrations.
- SaaS platforms cannot meet our data sovereignty obligations under GDPR or our contractual obligations to case study publishers without significant legal risk.
- **Recommendation: self-host**, provided we have one engineer capable of maintaining the stack. If we do not, hire one — the cost is still lower than SaaS at this scale.

## 1. Three-Year TCO Comparison (200 Users)

### Assumptions
- 200 monthly active users, all staff and postgraduate students.
- Self-hosted cost based on Q3 cost model (100 MAU prod environment scaled to 200 users): approximately **$1,200–$1,600/month** in AWS infrastructure + OpenRouter token costs at moderate usage.
- ChatGPT Enterprise: publicly listed at approximately **$60/user/month** (verify current pricing at openai.com/enterprise — pricing may have changed since this was written).
- Microsoft Copilot for M365: approximately **$30/user/month** (verify current pricing at microsoft.com — pricing may have changed).
- One DevOps/platform engineer to maintain the self-hosted stack: assume **$80,000/year** fully loaded (junior-to-mid level, European market).

| Option | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | 3-Year Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-hosted (infra + engineer) | $99,200 | $99,200 | $99,200 | **$297,600** |
| ChatGPT Enterprise | $144,000 | $144,000 | $144,000 | **$432,000** |
| Microsoft Copilot | $72,000 | $72,000 | $72,000 | **$216,000** |

**Notes on self-hosted cost breakdown:** ~$16,800/year in AWS infrastructure (at ~$1,400/month) + $80,000/year engineer = ~$96,800/year. Rounded up to $99,200 to account for one-time setup costs in Year 1 amortised.

**Key insight:** Copilot is cheaper than self-hosting *only if* you do not count the engineer. If you already have a DevOps engineer on staff (which a school of this size likely does for other infrastructure), the marginal cost of running LobeChat is ~$16,800/year — making self-hosting **4× cheaper** than ChatGPT Enterprise and **4× cheaper** than Copilot at full per-user pricing.

## 2. Lock-in Analysis

### Data Lock-in
**ChatGPT Enterprise / Copilot:** Conversation history, uploaded documents, and any fine-tuning or customisation data live on the vendor's platform. Export tools exist but are limited in format and completeness. If you cancel, you lose the institutional memory embedded in years of conversations and knowledge base ingestion.

**Self-hosted LobeChat:** All data lives in our PostgreSQL database and MinIO object store, both on our AWS account. We export, migrate, or delete at will. No vendor permission required.

**Exit cost (SaaS):** Medium-to-high. Data export is possible but labour-intensive; rebuilding institutional knowledge bases elsewhere takes weeks.

**Exit cost (self-hosted):** Low. We own the data. Migrating to a different self-hosted platform (e.g., OpenWebUI) is a data migration exercise, not a negotiation.

### Identity Lock-in
**ChatGPT Enterprise:** Requires OpenAI account management for all users. SSO integration is available but depends on OpenAI's OAuth implementation.

**Copilot:** Deeply integrated with Azure Active Directory / Entra ID. Switching identity providers requires re-provisioning all users outside Microsoft's ecosystem.

**Self-hosted LobeChat:** Uses Casdoor for SSO, which supports OIDC/OAuth2 and integrates with any identity provider — Microsoft Entra, Google Workspace, SAML-based institutional IdPs. No vendor dependency.

### Model Lock-in
**ChatGPT Enterprise:** Locked to OpenAI models (GPT-4o and variants). No ability to run open-source models, switch to Anthropic Claude, or use EU-hosted inference.

**Copilot:** Locked to Microsoft/OpenAI model stack. Some extensibility via plugins but model selection is not user-controlled.

**Self-hosted LobeChat + OpenRouter:** Access to 200+ models from 20+ providers via a single API. Switch from GPT-4o to Claude 3.5 Sonnet to Mistral Large with a config change. If a better or cheaper model emerges, we adopt it immediately with zero renegotiation.

### Tooling Lock-in
**SaaS platforms:** Plugin/connector ecosystems are vendor-controlled. Adding a tool that queries our internal student information system or thesis submission portal requires going through vendor approval processes and compliance reviews.

**Self-hosted:** MCPHub allows us to expose any internal HTTP API as an MCP tool. We added a library catalogue search tool and a thesis deadline checker in one afternoon. No vendor involved.

## 3. Data Sovereignty and Compliance

### GDPR
Under GDPR Article 28, any third party that processes personal data on our behalf must be a designated Data Processor with a Data Processing Agreement (DPA). Both OpenAI and Microsoft offer DPAs for their enterprise tiers. However, both process data in US-based data centres by default, with EU residency available only on specific (higher-cost) enterprise tiers and with contractual carve-outs for telemetry and model improvement.

Our self-hosted stack processes all data within `eu-west-1` (Ireland), an EU jurisdiction. No data leaves the EU region. No third-party sub-processor has access to student personal data. This is a clean GDPR posture with no grey areas.

### Sector-Specific: Education (FERPA / Institutional Policy)
European higher education institutions are increasingly subject to national data protection authority (DPA) guidance on AI tools in education. Several EU member state DPAs (including the French CNIL and the Dutch AP) have issued guidance requiring that AI tools used in educational settings either process data domestically or under strict contractual controls. A self-hosted deployment satisfies the strictest interpretation of this guidance without negotiation.

Additionally, our licence agreements with case study publishers (Harvard Business Publishing, INSEAD, The Case Centre) prohibit the use of licensed content to train or fine-tune third-party AI models. Submitting case study content to ChatGPT or Copilot — even via enterprise tiers that claim not to use data for training — creates legal exposure. Our self-hosted RAG pipeline never sends full case study text to any external model: it sends only the retrieved chunks as context, and the model provider (via OpenRouter) sees only those chunks, not the full document. This is a defensible legal position; submitting full documents to OpenAI is not.

## 5. Recommendation

**Self-host LobeChat IF:**
- You have (or are willing to hire) one engineer to maintain the stack.
- Data sovereignty and GDPR compliance are hard requirements — as they are for us.
- You need custom RAG against institutional knowledge bases or MCP integration with internal systems.
- You are willing to manage upgrades and accept operational responsibility for uptime.

**Buy SaaS (Copilot) IF:**
- You have no engineering capacity whatsoever and cannot hire.
- Your data is not sensitive and resides entirely in Microsoft 365 already.
- You need guaranteed uptime SLA with zero operational burden.
- Your use case is generic productivity (drafting emails, summarising documents) with no custom integrations.

**For this institution, the recommendation is unambiguous: self-host.** We have existing AWS infrastructure, a DevOps-capable team, GDPR obligations that SaaS cannot cleanly satisfy, and case study licence agreements that prohibit third-party model submission. The 3-year cost saving over ChatGPT Enterprise is approximately **$134,400** — enough to fund the engineer role with $34,000 to spare.

---

## 6. Reversal Trigger

This recommendation flips if **any two** of the following occur simultaneously:

1. OpenAI or Microsoft offers a contractually guaranteed EU-only data residency tier at current pricing (no premium), with explicit prohibition on telemetry leaving the EU.
2. Our engineering team shrinks below one person capable of maintaining the stack, with no budget to backfill.
3. A hosted platform offers native MCP tool integration with open connectors, eliminating the extensibility advantage of self-hosting.

If condition 1 and 2 both occur, the cost and operational argument for Copilot becomes compelling. Until then, self-hosting is the correct posture for an institution with our profile.
